Executive Summary

Inside Big Tech: Developer AI Usage & Engineering Output

Verified disclosures from 53 companies, from Google, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta to HubSpot, Airbnb, Uber, IBM, Bank of America and Baidu, on how internal AI adoption translated into code share, pull request throughput, modernization velocity, and workforce capacity. Every figure was checked word-for-word against the company's own words; claims found only in news reports were left out.

Core Discovery: The Bottleneck Inversion
AI is writing a fast-growing share of code. Google's AI-generated share of new code rose from >25% (Oct 2024) to 75% (Apr 2026), and Zeta Global reports 89.6% (Q2 2026). But code share is not shipping output: almost no company ties these numbers to cycle time or delivery throughput. Our reading of the evidence is that the human constraint has shifted to code review, automated testing, and architectural validation.

Productivity Evidence

Where Productivity Is Actually Measured

The chart above, and the Disclosure Register below, measure adoption: how much code AI writes. This one collects every verified figure that measures what AI does to output, grouped from the strongest evidence (what ships, and how fast) to the weakest (a "productivity" number the company never defines). Each quote was re-checked against its source on 13 Oct 2026.

What it shows: most evidence is activity per engineer (more PRs, commits or code changes), which can rise without anything shipping faster. Only three companies report a delivery outcome, and none reports standard delivery metrics such as lead time or deployment frequency. Telemetry from Faros AI (10,000+ developers) found no significant link between AI adoption and company-level delivery.

What independent research found

Checked but not included, because no company document or ruling could be verified: Commonwealth Bank reinstating 45 roles after an AI voice bot (media and union only), McDonald's ending its AI drive-thru test (statement to press only), Taco Bell keeping humans "in the loop" (press interview only), Cursor's support bot inventing a policy (the company's reply was a since-deleted Reddit post), IBM tripling entry-level hiring (press only), the Klarna CEO's "lower quality" remark (press interview), and AWS outages attributed to an AI agent (Amazon's own statement says AI was not the cause).
